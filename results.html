A collaboration between the University of Washington, PWFSL and the US Forest Service

By Mateo Alliende

A spatial model of post-fire high-regeneration probability across ~37,150 acres of forested burn on Monroe Mountain, with companion surfaces for pre-fire aspen presence (via phenology metrics), burn severity (RdNBR), and pre-fire aspen density (stem counts).

An improved understanding of how fuels and treatments contributed to this wildfire, as well as how the forest can be expected to recover, will aid future forest and fire management in the western United States.

Section 02

Threshold sensitivity

Area classified as high-regen at successive probability cutoffs.

This model is sensitive to probability thresholds, which can be set according to management goals and priorities. The Youden threshold (0.73) maximizes sensitivity and specificity against the validation data, but a lower threshold may be desirable to capture more potential high-regen areas (at the cost of more false positives).

Section 03

Confidence zones

Splitting the surface into five probability bands — high confidence high regen, moderate confidence high regen, uncertain but likely regen, uncertain but unlikely regen, and high confidence low regen.

Confidence zones can help managers understand the spatial distribution of model uncertainty and identify areas where additional monitoring or adaptive management may be most valuable.

Section 06

Inside the uncertain but likely zone

Character of the 10,310-acre uncertain-but-likely zone — burn severity, phenology, and aspen signal.

This section focuses on the 0.55–0.73 probability band, where regeneration is uncertain but more likely than not. Managers may want to prioritize monitoring and adaptive management in this interval to better understand recovery trajectories.

  • Pixels with high severity (>0.5) 99.7%

Section 07

Zone comparison — RdNBR & phenology

Mean values across the five confidence bands.

The high-confidence-low band stands out because it combines lower modeled regeneration probability with milder burn severity and a stronger phenological signal than the rest of the uncertain spectrum. In contrast, the high-confidence-high and moderate-confidence-high bands occupy steep, severely burned terrain that still retains strong post-fire regeneration potential, while the uncertain-but-likely band sits between these outcomes and retains enough aspen signal to make recovery plausible without the same certainty.

Across the landscape, ~46% of pixels exceed 0.50 probability
